Slide 2. Our Role Changes as They Grow

  1. Childhood

    We make many decisions for them.

  2. Teen years

    We guide them while they practice making decisions.

  3. Young adults

    We offer advice, support, and respect for their choices.

Our role changes. Our love does not.

Introduction

Slide 3. Why Do Teens Want So Much Independence?

During the teen years, young people are:

  • Learning who they are
  • Forming their own opinions
  • Caring more about friends
  • Practicing making decisions
  • Wanting more privacy and freedom

Wanting independence does not mean they no longer love or need us.

Introduction

Slide 4. Their Brains Are Still Growing

Brain science

The last part to mature

The part of the brain that plans ahead and makes careful decisions keeps maturing into the mid-to-late 20s.

Our job is to help them become responsible adults, not to keep them dependent on us forever.

Slide 7. Your Teen Still Needs You

Connection

“I know my parents love me and will be there when I need them.”

Independence

“My parents trust me to learn, grow, and make my own decisions.”

Psychology calls this

Secure attachment

A trusting bond that lets teens stay close and grow independent.

Step 1 · Connect

Slide 8. Listen Before You Lecture

When your teen comes to you with a problem:

  1. Listen without interrupting.
  2. Try to understand their feelings.
  3. Ask questions before giving advice.
  4. Stay calm, even if you disagree.

Try asking

“Do you want my advice, or do you just need me to listen?”

Step 2 · Give room to grow

Slide 10. Freedom with Boundaries

Healthy rules for teens are:

  • Clear
  • Fair
  • Right for their age
  • Explained
  • Loosened as trust grows

Example

Your 15-year-old wants to visit a friend.

“I need to know where you’ll be, who will be there, and how you’ll get home.”

Freedom grows as responsibility grows.

Step 2 · Give room to grow

Slide 11. Young Adults: From Control to Guidance

Teenagers

  • Need clear safety rules
  • Practice making decisions
  • Need guidance and supervision

Young adults

  • Make their own decisions
  • Need respect and independence
  • Still value advice and support

They should treat us with respect. We should respect their growing responsibility for their own lives.

Step 2 · Give room to grow

Slide 12. Kindness, Even in Disagreement

Disagreement is not the same as disrespect, for children and for parents.

Step 2 · Give room to grow

Slide 13. How Trust Grows

Trust grows when we:

  • Respect reasonable privacy
  • Ask questions instead of accusing
  • Agree on clear safety rules
  • Don’t check phones without a reason
  • Stay calm when they are honest

We want our teens to tell us things because they trust us, not only because they know we are watching.

Safety comes first

Step 3 · Guide with kindness

Slide 15. Five Questions for Parents of Teens

  1. Is our home a safe place?
  2. Do I notice and praise good choices?
  3. Are our rules clear and fair?
  4. Are my expectations realistic?
  5. Am I taking care of myself, too?

Based on research

These come from Triple P, the Positive Parenting Program. It has a version made for parents of teenagers.

Source: CrimeSolutions; Triple P

Step 3 · Guide with kindness

Slide 16. When Emotions Run High

We cannot always control what our children do. But we can work on how we respond.

Step 3 · Guide with kindness

Slide 17. Calm First, Then Talk

  1. Pause. Say “A‘udhu billah.” Sit down. Bukhari 6115 · Abu Dawud 4782
  2. Lower your voice. Don’t yell back.
  3. Take a break. Come back when you are both calm.
  4. Solve the problem together.

Try saying

“We’re both upset. Let’s talk when we’re calmer.”

Step 3 · Guide with kindness

Slide 18. Guilt and Shame Are Different

Guilt

“I did something wrong.”

It helps us admit mistakes and make things right.

Shame

“Something is wrong with me.”

It makes us feel worthless or afraid of rejection.

Correct the mistake without making your child feel like a bad person.

Step 3 · Guide with kindness

Slide 19. Words That Shame

Shaming words can sound like:

  • “You have brought shame to this family.”
  • “What will people think of us?”
  • “Look at how successful your cousin is.”
  • “You’re such a disappointment.”
  • “After everything we’ve done for you…”
  • “A good Muslim boy or girl would never do this.”

Slide 24. Keeping Them Close to Islam

How can we help our children love their faith?

  • Be a good example
  • Welcome honest questions
  • Explain the reasons behind our practice
  • Speak with kindness
  • Never use religion to shame them

We want them to build their own relationship with Allah, not to practice out of fear of us.

Step 3 · Guide with kindness

Slide 25. When They Ask Hard Questions

Your teen asks: “Why do I have to pray?”

✕ Instead of✓ Try

✕Instead of: “Don’t question me. Just do what you’re told!”

✓Try: “I’m glad you asked. Let’s talk about why salah matters.”

Don’t know the answer?

Say so. Then look for it together, or ask a kind teacher or imam.

Step 4 · Make things right

Slide 26. Saying Sorry Does Not Make You Weak

When parents apologize, they teach:

  • Everyone makes mistakes.
  • Respect goes both ways.
  • When we hurt someone, we fix it.
  • Relationships matter more than pride.

Research · 288 teens · 2025

A real apology from a parent led to more forgiveness and a better relationship. Parents did not lose authority.

Step 4 · Make things right

Slide 27. What a Real Apology Sounds Like

Example

You yelled at your adult child about their future.

“I’m sorry I yelled. I was worried about your future, but I should not have spoken to you that way. You have a right to your own thoughts. Next time, I will listen more calmly.”

You can apologize and still disagree with their choice.

Step 4 · Make things right

Slide 28. Leave These Words Out

Don’t add

  • “But you made me angry.”
  • “You know how much I’ve sacrificed for you.”
  • “You should be grateful I care so much.”

These words take the apology away.

Give them time

Your child does not have to forgive you right away. An apology means the most when real change follows.
