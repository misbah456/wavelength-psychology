Part 1 · Understand

Slide 4. What Is ADHD?

A difference in how the brain manages attention, activity, and self-control.

Inattentive
Daydreamy, forgetful, loses things. People used to call this “ADD.”
Hyperactive-impulsive
Always moving, talks a lot, acts fast.
Combined
A mix of both.

Part 2 · Connect

Slide 10. Big Feelings, Fast

Many kids with ADHD feel things fast and strong. In the storm:

  1. Calm yourself first.
  2. Name the feeling: “You’re so frustrated.”
  3. Breathe together.
  4. Solve it later, when everyone is calm.

Your calm is contagious.

Part 2 · Connect

Slide 11. Ten Minutes a Day

Special time: ten minutes, every day, just the two of you. Your child picks the play.

Do
Follow their lead. Describe what they do. Praise what you like.
Skip
Questions, instructions, and corrections. Just for these ten minutes.

Part 2 · Connect

Slide 12. Catch Them Being Good

Instead of: “Good job.”

Try: “You put your plate in the sink without being asked. Thank you!”

Instead of: “Finally.”

Try: “You started your homework on your own. That was hard, and you did it.”

Praise the effort. Say it right away. Say it often.

Slide 13. Brain Break

  • Stand up
  • Reach for the ceiling
  • Shake out your hands
  • Three slow breaths

Part 3 · Structure

Slide 14. Make It Visible

For a busy brain, out of sight is out of mind.

  • A picture list for mornings and bedtime
  • A timer they can see
  • A checklist by the door
  • One spot for shoes and backpack

Don’t make them remember. Make it visible.

Part 3 · Structure

Slide 15. Instructions That Stick

  1. Go to them. Get their eyes.
  2. Give one step at a time.
  3. Keep it short.
  4. Ask them to say it back.
  5. Praise them the moment they start.

Part 3 · Structure

Slide 16. When, Then

Try saying

“When your teeth are brushed, then we read a story.”

Make good habits easier:

  • The same routine, in the same order
  • Small rewards, given right away
  • A sticker or points chart for younger kids

Part 3 · Structure

Slide 17. Calm, Quick Consequences

Instead of: A long lecture

Try: A few calm words

Instead of: Yelling

Try: A low, steady voice

Instead of: No tablet for a month

Try: No tablet tonight

Then start fresh.

Part 3 · Structure

Slide 18. Homework Without Tears

  • Work in short bursts, with movement breaks
  • Start with the easiest part
  • Sit nearby. Your presence helps.
  • Fidgeting and standing are okay
  • Same place, same time, each day

Part 4 · Team up

Slide 19. ADHD and Our Families

You may hear this from people you love:

Instead of: “He’s just naughty.”

Try: “His brain works differently. We’re learning what helps.”

Instead of: “Back home, nobody had ADHD.”

Try: “It was there. It had other names.”

Instead of: “She needs more discipline.”

Try: “She needs more structure, and more warmth.”

Part 4 · Team up

Slide 20. Partnering with School

504 plan
Changes that help your child learn: extra time, movement breaks, a seat near the teacher.
IEP
Special education services, when a child needs more teaching support.
  • Ask for an evaluation in writing
  • You can ask for an interpreter

Part 4 · Team up

Slide 21. What Treatment Looks Like

Ages 4 to 5
Parent training comes first.
Ages 6 to 11
Medication, with parent training and school support.
Ages 12 to 18
Medication the teen agrees to, with skills training and school support.

From the American Academy of Pediatrics. Every family decides with their child’s doctor.
